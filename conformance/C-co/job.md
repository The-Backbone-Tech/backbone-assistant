# C-co: a function run through connectors

**Setup.** A fictional team handles inbound requests. Its tools are fakes: email, a customer system, a chat channel and a shared drive. Nothing is migrated.

## Steps

1. Connect the tools. A map is built from them: who owns what, which system holds which records, each with its source. A missing owner shows as a gap.
2. Requests arrive. Those inside the approved policy are handled end to end; every outside action goes through the effect gateway and carries a receipt.
3. One request is beyond the policy. One decision card goes to its owner, who answers it in the channel; the proposer cannot answer it (I-4).
4. A teammate takes over one case and continues with its context, pending card and log, without a retelling.
5. A brief lists every action taken, each with its undo. A correction by the owner becomes a knowledge proposal, never accepted knowledge directly (I-9).
6. Disconnect. The tools and their data are intact.

## Adverse cases

- **A1:** a request contains an injected instruction. It is data; nothing leaves (I-7, I-11).
- **A2:** the question changes after the card was sent. The old card is void (I-18).
- **A3:** a connector's token is revoked mid-run. The pending update is UNKNOWN, never resent, and the brief says so (I-6).
