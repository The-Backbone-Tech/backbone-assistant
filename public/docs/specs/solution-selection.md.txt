## Outcome

Given a real task, Backbone can use an existing native workflow, compose maintained pieces or create an authorised improvement when that is the best supported feasible way to produce the result. It completes and checks the work rather than stopping at a list of recommendations. The person can inspect, correct, export and continue the result. Verify the substance of the requested deliverable as well as its format. A proposed message should contain the wording its reviewer is meant to approve, with intended audience and current delivery state explicit. Resolve a material ambiguity from the task and sources, or ask a focused question while independent work continues. Label inferred responsibilities when no named owner is supplied.

**Scope clarification, 5 November 2026:** this capability is part of the complete Backbone platform defined by [GOAL](../../GOAL.md). Choosing an adequate incumbent for one company does not cancel platform development. Reusable configurations distinguish platform requirements, configuration-specific requirements, optional enhancements, alternatives and the selected arrangement as defined in [Shape](../shape.md#complete-platform-and-proportionate-configurations). Commercial products and services are allowed; their revenue must not bias design or selection.

**Owner clarification, 3 November 2026:** task completion serves continuing personal/team/company work. Recommend the best next step and operating arrangement for the company's transformation moment, actual stack, outcomes and capacity or desire to operate it. Compare whole-function value over setup, repeated use, corrections, shared handover, change, maintenance, recovery and exit. Existing, native, free/community, assisted, paid and fully managed solutions are eligible; monetisation and Backbone itself receive no preference. A managed service can win by removing enough implementation and operating burden. Retaining an incumbent or removing our mechanism may be the best result. A single completed task or benchmark rank does not establish this continuing value.

For example, a renewal-exception brief may use permitted source records, exact calculations, an editable report and unsent drafts. Different available tools, permissions, privacy constraints or budgets can make different approaches appropriate. The existing synthetic renewal study is a useful structured-data control; it does not establish live vendor superiority or prose quality.

## Information and decisions

1. **Understand the job.** Identify the intended deliverable, current transformation needs, operating capacity, material constraints and any missing fact that can change the outcome. Discover what is actually available before asking for information the environment already supplies.
2. **Establish feasibility.** Distinguish eligible current routes from a possible setup, new entitlement or missing authority. Preserve the actual person/account, operation, data scope, quota and cost uncertainty. Credentials remain with their proper holder; a product being installed or signed in is not proof of entitlement.
3. **Consult relevant evidence.** Arena or another replaceable provider can supply dated sources, capabilities, limits, alternatives and provenance. A record or popularity score is not task-fit, current authority or proof that a vendor works in this environment. Stale, conflicting or absent evidence stays visible.
4. **Choose proportionately.** Consider the feasible existing approach and any useful adaptation or new work. Compare expected task quality, time, human effort, setup, ongoing ownership, recovery and exit, subject to hard constraints. Give a short reason and meaningful uncertainty; equally defensible choices may remain tied. A simple task need not trigger a large survey or a fixed multi-agent ceremony.
5. **Execute and verify.** Use the selected maintained tools or built-in mechanisms through the existing authority and effect boundaries. Bind the chosen route to the admitted work and check the effective route against the applicable access, data-transfer and spend constraints at execution. If it changes, replan within those constraints or report the dependency before transferring data or spending. Check the actual deliverable and retain the relevant sources and outcomes. Follow [continuing-work](continuing-work.md) for edits, interruption and actions with unknown outcomes.

Check the recommendation against the effective request and verify the resulting version. The following observations belong to the same acceptance journey, with distinct controls.

| What the product claims | Observation that can support it |
| --- | --- |
| This route is eligible for this work. | Compare the selected route's actual destination, identity, data scope and spend with the task constraints. A common engine or model name does not establish that binding. |
| This execution followed the recommendation. | Observe the request at the admitted destination. In a paired case with a changed or disallowed route, observe zero requests at the disallowed destination and a clear reason for holding or selecting another eligible route. |
| This result came from that execution. | Join the execution and receipt to the exact written version. A later human revision remains current and is identified separately from what the agent wrote. |
| This work achieved the intended result. | Check the bound result against the task's evidence and acceptance criteria. A readable artifact and a valid receipt establish custody and execution facts, while correctness requires this separate check. |

When the evidence leaves the long-term choice open, recommend a reversible step that serves the current job or resolves the decisive uncertainty. Say which purpose it serves. For example, continue a checked manual export when it meets the deadline, and test an unattended export on one approved cycle if the decision turns on recurring effort. If two eligible routes both meet the outcome and the difference is immaterial, keep the familiar route or let the operator's preference decide. If an unknown could change a consequential commitment, keep that commitment pending and identify who can settle it. Revisit the broader choice when the observation arrives. This applies Base's adopt, adapt, watch and reject dispositions without requiring a final winner in every comparison.

Private operating context and decisions stay with the tenant's owners. External evidence can be reused without exporting private task data. A proposed improvement has an explicit source and licence where relevant, a description of what changed, and an outcome comparison; it may be rejected if it fails or costs more to own.

## Removing obstacles during execution

The agent retains responsibility for the requested outcome when a preferred route fails. It discovers available capabilities, inspects errors and dependencies, repairs the environment within its mandate and tries an eligible alternative when needed. Expose actionable failures through the existing task, tool and connection surfaces: the affected operation, what is known about its outcome, the useful next step and any decision or access actually required. Preserve completed work and resume at the affected point after the dependency is resolved.

For example, an agent preparing a sourced report may find an unfamiliar export tool, then encounter a missing local parser. It should discover the tool's supported inputs and outputs, inspect the export and use or prepare an appropriate parser in its authorised workspace. If that requires installing a dependency, apply the workspace's existing network, code-execution and dependency rules. It can also choose an available native reader or exact calculation. If the remaining obstacle is permission to retrieve one attachment, prepare the independent sections and request the specific access or approved input needed for that section. The requested report stays open until its required contents are verified.

Use the existing acceptance journey to distinguish successful adaptation from unnecessary blocking. Include a required capability available through discovery, a recoverable setup failure and a changed tool response. Observe whether the agent reaches the complete result, how much human rescue and repeated work it needs, and whether the resulting work is correct. Pair these with an operation outside the permission: the agent should preserve useful work and identify a valid way to obtain the missing decision. A polished partial answer or a safe refusal alone cannot establish completion. Existing frozen acceptance criteria retain their owning method; these are proof questions for the product behavior.

Estimate what happens if the company keeps its current arrangement for the same comparison period. Existing licences can make incremental spend low while recurring repair, missed deadlines or a fragile manual dependency remain costly. Conversely, a projected gain may arrive after the need changes or fail to cover training and transition. Compare waiting, a reversible improvement and a deeper change when each is credible. Separate what has already been spent from what the company will still spend or have to do. If a brief trial can resolve the decisive uncertainty before the cost of waiting matters, run it. If waiting would miss the useful window, choose the best eligible step now and record the uncertainty it leaves.

Compare the cost of changing the arrangement as well as running it. A bundled workspace can reduce the number of accounts, integrations and support owners today. A separate provider can make sense if it removes an important dependency or can take over one operation without moving work state. Count that benefit only when a plausible change has been exercised or can be costed from actual dependencies. If both routes require the same underlying permission, adding a second vendor does not remove that dependency. ## Worked decision: a new company workflow

Consider a company that uses a CRM for account records, Microsoft 365 for documents, and a managed identity provider. Its support team needs a weekly exception brief. Start with authorized read-only retrieval, exact rules for measurable facts, and an editable draft in the existing workspace. The CRM remains authoritative for accounts, the document workspace owns human revisions, and the acting principal retains the source access that actually applies to them. Pick the native route if the actual licence, source visibility and editing workflow cover the job. A read-only connector and deterministic calculation become reasonable when native retrieval or exact reconciliation fails. Introduce a coordinator only if recurring reassignment or asynchronous work makes the incumbent materially harder to operate. A managed service can win if the operator can demonstrate the same controls and spend less time on upkeep.

Test this arrangement with the original operator and a successor who has narrower access. The successor must see the correct document revision and only permitted source material. Revoke a permission between planning and execution. Confirm that the next attempted operation is denied and that the operator can still find the last valid result. Treat a CRM write as a separate decision. Its authorization and receipt must identify the actor, resource, intended effect and operation. Simulate loss of acknowledgement after a commit, then establish the actual outcome at the CRM before retrying. Compare the incumbent and the candidate over two cycles using accepted output, correction time, setup, handover, incident handling and export. Stop at the read-only route if the write does not earn its operational burden.

The design decision is conditional. If the licensed workspace already serves both operators with clear provenance and stable exports, keep it. If task ownership repeatedly disappears across tools, compare one coordinator against that incumbent. If the managed route cannot prove revocation or export for the affected scope, keep the relevant operations on an eligible existing route. Record what changed the choice so another person or agent can revisit it.

Keep that stack constant and vary the work. A team that needs a reliable Friday brief and can rotate its preparation among existing licensed staff should keep the attended route while it meets the deadline. A team with the same subscriptions and a daily deadline may need an authorized unattended source connection and a named owner for failures. First verify whether its existing automation can meet those conditions. If it can, keep it. If the missing duty is ongoing support rather than workflow execution, compare a managed support arrangement before replacing the workflow. If the deadline changes again, reassess that duty before buying additional capacity. These are hypothetical choices that test whether the recommendation responds to operating needs rather than the stack label.

**Desk check for partial access.** Keep the weekly brief and the existing CRM and document workspace. Assume the team's current AI seats allow attended drafting, the automation account can read only an approved CRM view, and the source owner has withheld access to contract attachments. Choose a route for each operation.

The same seat can therefore be useful to the author and insufficient for an unattended run. A paid integration is worth comparing if it can take on the actual missing operation and recurring duty. **Desk check for selective use.** Assume a specialist saves 12 minutes on each of 10 difficult cases in a batch of 100, adds two minutes of transfer and checking to every routed case, and produces no gain on routine cases. Sending every case adds 80 minutes net. A routing rule that sends 20 cases, including eight difficult ones, saves 56 minutes before the cost of identifying those cases. ## Experiments that can change the choice

Arena maintains dated comparisons and conditional picks. A lab tests a bounded proposition against an eligible incumbent. A simulation exercises a planned change or adverse sequence with explicit fixture and runtime scope. Exact checks belong where the outcome has a known answer. Calibrate judgment of usefulness with people who own the work. Qualification requires the selected installed arrangement, with its actual authority, records and operators.

Choose measures that answer the decision before seeing results. Separate the outcome the recipient values, the conditions every eligible route must satisfy and the observations that explain a difference. For an exception brief, the outcome can be correct action before the deadline, with current access and an editable result as conditions. Token use, drafts produced and operator interventions help explain cost or friction. If a faster route causes more missed exceptions, inspect the consequence before treating speed as a win. If both meet the same outcome and conditions, the operating measures can decide. Change the rubric when the business question changes, and keep earlier results attributable to their original question.

Distinguish discovering the right problem from choosing a route for a known problem. With the facts fixed, a comparison can test whether the selector makes a better choice from the same eligible options. With the facts initially incomplete, test whether it discovers the consequential missing fact or asks the right person, and whether that changes the recommendation. For example, disclose after the initial request that the report already exists and that cases stall while a team waits for a policy decision. A faster report generator can be an excellent answer to the wrong problem. A diagnostic comparison can expose that even before running a new tool. The complete-job comparison then checks whether the resulting arrangement actually helps. This adds a research question alongside the owning J-solution comparisons, which distinguish same-model selection from an end-to-end result.

| Hypothesis | Test and reason to revise |
| --- | --- |
| A transferable record reduces the cost of handover | Give a successor the incumbent materials and the candidate record in comparable cases. Measure time to resume, missed constraints and preserved human changes. Keep the incumbent if the extra record creates equivalent rework. |
| A new coordinator reduces the cost of recurring shared work | Compare repeated work and recovery with one authoritative work record in each arm. Decline the extra service if review, reassignment and recovery gains fail to cover identity, upkeep and exit costs. |
| A model or engine can be replaced without breaking the supported job | Replace one effective route, preserve actor and artifact identity, and repeat both the normal journey and its adverse case. Limit the portability claim to the boundary exercised. |
| Learned guidance improves later work without weakening access controls | Run a versioned skill on held-out cases, including revoked access and stale context. Retire it if the gain disappears after review or it encourages boundary violations. |

## Cases the implementation must handle

- A native or already licensed tool is usable for one person but unavailable or not authorised for another.
- A requested service needs additional spend, account consent or a data transfer; independent work continues within existing authority where possible.
- Evidence is missing, stale or contradictory; no record is not proof that no option exists.
- Access, quota or connectivity changes during work. Before admission, verify that any declared fallback uses an eligible identity, data destination and payer, and record which route actually ran. After possible dispatch, keep the original operation and resolver, and classify its outcome before retrying. Independent work with eligible inputs can continue. Preserve the person's saved edits and pending effects.
- Two routes share an engine. Select the local route for a restricted task while a metered route is also configured. Observe the effective model and destination, then change the configured route before admission. Require the selected eligible route or an explicit limitation, with no request to the disallowed destination.
- A saved routine becomes due after the host changes its model route. Recheck the effective account, data destination and payer for that invocation before sending inputs. Exercise an eligible replacement and a route outside the routine's allowed scope. The former can continue, and the latter must receive no request and leave a visible dependency. A fixed-route schedule test and an interactive route-selection test cover different parts.
- Source content attempts to issue instructions or widen authority; seeing it does not authorise an action.
- A tool reports success but the actual deliverable is absent or unverifiable; do not report completion.
- A turn writes version 1 and the person saves materially different version 2 before verification. Check the original write against its own receipt and version, preserve version 2 as current, and report the distinction. If version 1 is unavailable, report that limitation. Reading version 2 or matching the filename cannot verify version 1.
- A preferred executor becomes unavailable. Check which parts another eligible route can finish, including exact calculation and an assisted review. Where a source or governing rule is missing, identify the claims that depend on it. Deliver verified independent work with the missing part and its next owner visible, and mark completion only when the requested outcome has been checked. If no eligible route can make useful progress, name the concrete dependency. For comparison, count complete and partial work separately and include the operator effort each requires.

## Evidence and acceptance

The existing J-solution design calls for incoming-request triage, an editable report, and a bounded transformation across native/licensed, API/OSS and restricted profiles, plus held-out work. The exact fixtures, eligible routes, budgets and method are frozen before a scored run. Only available environments count as live product tests; unavailable products remain untested. The contract requires at least one completion case per profile, and a partial matrix is not acceptance.

Use a matched model/route, tools, authority and budget to isolate selection's contribution, and separately compare the best-permitted complete approaches. Measure outcome quality, critical errors, human rework, elapsed time, setup, maintenance, recovery, exit and total cost. A derived candidate must be accepted, revised or rejected against its own hypothesis. Public benchmarks and vendor claims can inform the candidates; they do not replace this comparison or the installed continuing-work and adverse cases.

For a skill or engine change, compare the maintained baseline with the same useful information as well as without the added mechanism; otherwise extra context can masquerade as a method advantage. Freeze the strongest relevant eligible benchmark challenger and its observed version/settings before scoring, separately from the whole operating comparison. Preserve held-out work and uncertainty; a model–engine–effort leaderboard row does not isolate the engine. Qualify later repetition, changed rules/access and shared operation over the owning job's stated horizon, while preserving frozen graders and acceptance.

When a selected approach processes structured results in code or reduces model-visible output, verify the exact calculations against the source fixture and preserve source/version and coverage information. Distinguish a verified empty extract from missing or unverified coverage. Label incomplete totals and check uniqueness against the source identity and version, preserving legitimate records that share a display name. Check the context actually admitted to the model as well as the resulting artifact/state, including a withdrawn source copied into derived text. Reuse the existing material, effect and evaluation owners; these observations refine the affected trial rather than add another acceptance service or universal per-task checklist.

This specification does not select a universal provider, require a hosted Arena service, create a company-wide mirror, mandate model training, or authorise purchases or external effects.
